Where to take paint, chemicals, batteries, and other hazardous waste in the Inland Empire

Updated October 2026. General information, not legal advice.

Most junk removal crews can't legally take household hazardous waste (HHW) to a regular landfill, and California bans throwing many of these items in the trash. Both counties in the Inland Empire run free programs for residents.

What counts as household hazardous waste

  • Paint, stains, and solvents
  • Motor oil, oil filters, and antifreeze
  • Batteries (California prohibits putting any batteries in the trash)
  • Pesticides, pool chemicals, and cleaners
  • Fluorescent bulbs
  • Propane cylinders (ask the facility)

San Bernardino County

San Bernardino County Fire runs household hazardous waste collection facilities that are free for county residents. Proof of residency may be requested, and business waste isn't accepted. Check the county's facility list for the nearest site and its hours, which include a central site at San Bernardino International Airport and a Saturday site at the Redlands city yard.

Riverside County

The Riverside County Department of Waste Resources runs free permanent HHW facilities (including Agua Mansa near Riverside) and temporary collection events. There's a limit of 15 gallons or 125 pounds per resident per trip.

Tips

  • Keep products in their original containers with lids on, and never mix chemicals.
  • Transport containers upright and secured, away from passengers if possible.
  • Tell your junk removal provider about any HHW in advance so they can quote the rest of the job without it.

Common questions

Will junk removal companies take paint?

Usually not. Most can't take wet paint or other hazardous waste. Use your county's free HHW program.

Is hazardous waste drop-off free in Riverside County?

Yes, for residents, at county HHW facilities and events, with a limit of 15 gallons or 125 pounds per trip.
