How much does junk removal cost in the Inland Empire?

Updated October 2026. General information, not legal advice.

We don't publish price ranges, because we haven't found a reliable, current, Inland Empire-specific source, and national averages can be misleading. Here's what actually drives the price so you can compare quotes fairly. Remember that a few items may be free through your city. See our bulky pickup guide.

The main cost factors

  • Volume. Many haulers price by how much of the truck your items fill (a few items, a quarter load, a full load).
  • Weight and material. Concrete, dirt, tile, roofing, and other dense materials cost more to haul and dispose of, and may need a different truck or several trips.
  • Labor and access. Items inside, upstairs, or far from the truck take longer than a curbside pile. Mention stairs and long carries.
  • Disassembly. Hot tubs, sheds, playsets, and built-ins need to be taken apart first.
  • Special handling. Appliances with refrigerant, mattresses, tires, and electronics may carry extra disposal or recycling fees.
  • Timing. Same-day or weekend service may cost more.

How to compare quotes

  1. Send the same photos and description to each provider.
  2. Ask whether the quote is a firm price or an estimate that can change when they see the load.
  3. Ask what's included: loading, sweeping, disposal fees, and recycling or donation.
  4. Ask for proof of insurance, and ask where the load will go.

Common questions

Is junk removal priced by weight or volume?

Usually by volume (how much truck space you fill), with extra charges for heavy materials or special items. Ask each provider how they price.

Can I get a junk removal quote from photos?

Many providers will quote from clear photos and a description, then confirm on site.
