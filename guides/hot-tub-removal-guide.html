Hot tub and spa removal: what to know before you hire

Updated October 2026. General information, not legal advice.

An old hot tub is one of the hardest things to get rid of yourself. Spas are heavy, bulky, and wired into your electrical system, and city bulky programs generally won't take them. Moreno Valley's bulky-item list, for example, explicitly excludes jacuzzi tubs and spas.

Step by step

  1. Disconnect power. Have a qualified electrician shut off and disconnect the spa's dedicated circuit before anyone starts cutting. Don't let a hauler cut live wiring.
  2. Drain it. Drain the water a day or two ahead. Check your city's rules on where pool and spa water can go. Many cities don't allow chlorinated water into storm drains.
  3. Whole removal or cut-up. If there's a clear path, some crews remove the spa whole on a dolly or trailer. Otherwise they cut it into sections on site.
  4. Haul and clean up. Make sure the quote covers debris, the cover and steps, and sweeping the pad.
  5. The pad. Removing a concrete pad is a separate, heavier job. Mention it if you want it gone.

What drives the price

  • Size and weight of the spa
  • Access: gates, stairs, slopes, and distance to the truck
  • Whether it's above-ground or built into a deck or the ground (in-ground or deck-built spas are demolition work)
  • Electrical disconnect (usually a separate electrician)
  • Concrete pad or deck removal

Licensing

Simple haul-away isn't usually contractor work, but tearing out a built-in spa, deck, or concrete can be. California requires a CSLB license for jobs of $1,000 or more in labor and materials (or any job needing a permit). Ask, and check the license at cslb.ca.gov.

Common questions

Will the city pick up my old hot tub?

Usually not. Moreno Valley's bulky program explicitly excludes spas, and most programs exclude items too heavy for the crew to lift safely. Hire a junk removal crew.

Do I need an electrician to remove a hot tub?

Yes, have a qualified electrician disconnect the spa's circuit before removal.
