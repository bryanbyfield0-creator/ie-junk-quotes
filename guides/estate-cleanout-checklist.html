Estate cleanout checklist: how to clear a loved one's home

Updated October 2026. General information, not legal advice.

Clearing out a parent's or relative's home is emotional and often time-sensitive, especially when the house is being sold or a rental lease is ending. This checklist covers the practical side.

Before anything leaves the house

  1. Confirm who has authority to remove property (executor, trustee, or the heirs together). When in doubt, ask the estate's attorney.
  2. Secure documents and valuables: wills and trust papers, deeds, titles, bank and tax records, jewelry, photos, keys, and passwords.
  3. Photograph rooms before sorting, for records and to settle family questions later.

Sort into four groups

  • Keep: items family members want. Label them and move them first.
  • Sell: valuable furniture, collectibles, and vehicles. Consider an appraiser or estate sale company.
  • Donate: usable furniture, clothing, and housewares.
  • Dispose: broken items, trash, and anything left over.

Use free options for part of the load

Your city's free bulky-item pickups can take some furniture and appliances. See our city-by-city guide. Paint, chemicals, and batteries should go to the county's free hazardous waste program.

Bringing in a cleanout crew

  • Ask whether they sort donations and recycling, and whether they provide donation receipts.
  • Ask how they handle papers and photos they find (set aside, never discarded).
  • Get a firm written price and scope, including sweeping out.
  • Ask for proof of insurance.

Common questions

Should I hire an estate sale company or a junk removal company?

Often both: an estate sale company to sell valuable items first, then a cleanout crew for what's left.

Can a junk removal crew donate items from an estate?

Many can set aside usable items for donation. Ask when you compare quotes.
